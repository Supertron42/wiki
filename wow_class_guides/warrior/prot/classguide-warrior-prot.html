WoW Classic Anniversary TBC - Protection Warrior Tank Guide

Your fast track to a raid-ready Protection Warrior in WoW Classic Anniversary (TBC). Pre-raid BiS, per-phase raid upgrades, gems, enchants, consumables, and the max-threat rotation, with every pick ranked by consensus across the major tank guides and checked against live item stats. Hover any item, boss, or ability for full Wowhead tooltips.

Overview

Protection Warriors are TBC's premier main tank: the strongest single-target threat, the deepest cooldown kit (Shield Wall, Last Stand, Shield Block), and the best physical mitigation in the game. Gear it right and you main-tank everything from Karazhan to Sunwell. Two numbers drive the whole build, sort these first:

1. Hit the Defense cap. 490 Defense (you start with 350 at level 70, so you need +140 from gear, talents, and enchants) makes you uncrittable by raid bosses. Priority #1 before stacking anything else.

2. Then follow the stat priority: Defense (to cap) > Stamina > Dodge/Parry/Block > Hit/Expertise (threat) > Block Value (trash/heroics).

Rotation & Threat

Tank threat is a priority system, not a fixed sequence: cast the highest item on the list that is available and affordable, then auto-attack. Maximising threat (and your own damage) comes down to never wasting a global cooldown and dumping spare Rage into Heroic Strike. Built from the Icy Veins and Warcraft Tavern Protection rotation guides.

The whole rotation in one line: Shield Block (defense) > Shield Slam > Revenge > Devastate filler, and spend leftover Rage on Heroic Strike. On a 6 sec cooldown, Shield Slam lands every 4th global, giving the classic loop Shield Slam > Revenge > Devastate > Devastate.

Single-Target Threat Priority

Top to bottom, use the first ability that is off cooldown and affordable:

  1. Shield Block on cooldown when taking heavy damage. It grants crush immunity while active, so keep it up on bosses. You may skip it if Rage-starved and you need threat, but that risks a crushing blow.
  2. Shield Slam on cooldown. Your single biggest threat ability, and it ignores armor.
  3. Revenge whenever it lights up (after a block, dodge or parry). Highest threat per Rage in the kit.
  4. Devastate as filler. It applies and refreshes Sunder Armor, so spam it at the start of a pull to stack the debuff to 5 quickly, then keep it rolling.
  5. Heroic Strike as your Rage dump. Ideally every auto-attack is queued as a Heroic Strike when Rage allows; it is "on next swing" and does not cost a global.

Heroic Strike vs Devastate: once Sunder Armor (or Expose Armor) is at full stacks and you have Rage to spare, Heroic Strike out-threats another Devastate. Devastate is your filler only when you cannot afford to dump Rage.

Pull & Opener

  1. Use Bloodrage between pulls to enter combat with a Rage pool. If you pull with a ranged weapon, use it just after combat starts so it spreads a little threat onto everything.
  2. If you are below ~10 Rage and the pack does not fear, drop to Berserker Stance and use Berserker Rage (more Rage from damage taken), then return to Defensive Stance.
  3. Open with a ranged shot pull, or Charge from Battle Stance into the pack. Land Thunder Clap for multi-mob packs or Shield Slam for one to two targets, then settle into Defensive Stance and run the priority above.

Multi-Target / AoE Threat

This is where Prot is weakest. Think of it as "juggling plates": track your threat lead on every mob and be ready to stun or taunt the one about to break loose.

  • 2 to 3 mobs: run the single-target priority and rotate your target every global so threat builds evenly. Tab through them rather than tunnelling one.
  • Thunder Clap on cooldown: solid AoE threat plus a 20% attack-speed slow (talented).
  • Cleave replaces Heroic Strike as your Rage dump on packs; it hits a second target.
  • Demoralizing Shout hits everything for threat and cuts enemy attack power (mitigation too).
  • Whirlwind (from Berserker Stance) adds burst AoE damage/threat on big pulls; use situationally since it leaves Defensive Stance.
  • Recover a loose mob with Taunt, Concussion Blow (stun + threat), or Challenging Shout for a whole-pack emergency.

Rage Management

  • Learn the Rage cost of each ability and keep your Rage bar where you can see it.
  • Reserve enough Rage to re-cast Shield Block the moment it falls off; do not spend yourself empty on Heroic Strike.
  • Think a couple of globals ahead: if Shield Block drops in 2 sec, bank the Rage for it now.
  • When Rage is flowing, dump everything into Heroic Strike (or Cleave on packs); idle Rage is wasted threat.
  • Use Bloodrage between pulls so you never start a fight empty.

Keep These Up

  • Whichever of Battle Shout or Commanding Shout you are assigned, for the whole fight.
  • Demoralizing Shout for the enemy attack-power reduction.
  • Thunder Clap for the attack-speed slow, on cooldown.
  • Five stacks of Sunder Armor (via Devastate) on the boss for the raid.

Questing Strategy

Quests reward gear, gold, rep, and XP all at once, but only if you batch them. The pickup-everything-first workflow saves hours of backtracking and turns every mob kill into 3-4 simultaneous progress bars (rep + quest + farm + Marks for Aldor turn-ins).

Universal rule: When you arrive at a quest hub or new zone, walk every NPC with a yellow ! before doing anything else. Then leave town with a full quest log (25 quests) and a plan that completes 4-6 quests per loop. Never kill or gather "just to grind", there's almost always a quest objective for that mob/node.

Phase 1 - Karazhan / Gruul's Lair / Magtheridon's Lair

Phase 1 is the T4 Warbringer Armor era plus off-set drops from Karazhan, Gruul and Magtheridon. The rankings below are the consensus across the major tank guides. Where the survival pick and the threat pick genuinely split, both are listed: take the survival item until you are crit-immune (490 Defense), then move to the threat item. The standout drop is the Aldori Legacy Defender shield from Gruul, which stays BiS for many phases.

T4 Set Bonuses (Warbringer Armor): 2pc = chance on parry to gain Blade Turning (absorbs 200 damage for 15 sec). 4pc = your Revenge makes your next damaging ability deal 10% more damage. The bonuses are weak, so several tier pieces are beaten by off-set, PvP or world-boss items. Tokens (Fallen Defender): Helm Prince Malchezaar, Gloves The Curator, Shoulders High King Maulgar, Legs Gruul, Chest Magtheridon.

Two warrior T4 sets share the same tokens: Warbringer Battlegear (the -plates/-helm/-gauntlets threat pieces) and Warbringer Armor (the -guards survival pieces). Once you are crit-immune the threat pieces win at most slots, so the threat version is the BiS and the survival version is the avoidance alternative.

Sourced from the Wowhead Protection Warrior Phase 2 BiS Guide. Phase 2 adds T5 (Destroyer Armor, the Warrior tank set) plus SSC/TK off-set drops. Tier tokens drop from SSC/TK bosses and turn in at Shattrath for the spec helm/shoulders/chest/gloves/legs. The Spyglass of the Hidden Fleet, which drops off Serpentshrine Cavern trash, is the standout new trinket, grab it early in your SSC clears.

T5 Set Bonuses (Destroyer Armor, the tank set): 2pc = each Shield Block grants +100 block value against a single attack for 6 sec. 4pc = 7% chance when you are hit to gain 200 haste rating for 10 sec. The 2-set is the bonus you plan around. The 4-set is a streaky threat bonus that does nothing for your survival, which is exactly why the slot you leave open should usually buy that survival back.

Keeping the 4-Set: Your One Free Tier Slot

Destroyer Armor is a five-piece set that only pays out at two and four pieces, so the fifth tier slot is already yours to spend. The question is never "do I break my 4-set", it is "which of the five slots do I leave open, and what do I put there". Everything below assumes the other four tier pieces stay on.

The Fury tier pieces are not "breaking" anything. Destroyer Battle-Helm and its siblings belong to Destroyer Battlegear, a separate set whose bonuses (Overpower attack power, cheaper Bloodthirst and Mortal Strike) a Prot warrior cannot use at all. So a DPS tier piece is not tier for you, it is simply a strong non-tier plate item that happens to share a token, and it is a completely legitimate way to spend the free slot.

Because the 4-set bonus is a threat proc, the free slot is usually where you buy your survival back. Open the slot where your best available drop lands, then re-check your Defense before you zone in.

Re-check 490 Defense after any of these swaps. Every one of the five tier pieces carries Defense rating, and most of the off-set replacements carry none at all. Opening the head or leg slot in particular can quietly drop you below crit immunity, and a crit-immune tank with less threat always beats a crittable tank with more. If a swap puts you under, make it back up with a Thick Dawnstone (+8 Defense Rating) or Enchant Bracer - Major Defense (+12) before you keep the piece.

Default plan: keep tier on shoulders, chest, and legs, then choose between hands and head for your fifth slot. Open hands if you want survival, open head if you want threat and your Defense can absorb it. Wearing the Fury helm for threat while the other four tier pieces hold your 4-set is a completely standard setup, not a mistake.

Standard Setup: What to Walk Into Phase 4 With

This is not the perfect setup. It is a balanced set. It is safe and it does good threat. But it is a starting point, not the best answer for every fight.

You can change it in two ways:

  • Add more threat gear. The set is 7 Defense skill above the cap, and it does not spend a single gem on Defense. If you find a better threat piece, you have room to take it.
  • Add more survival gear. Several slots still hold pure threat items. If a boss hits too hard, swap some out.

Pick what fits your goal: for high parses, add threat. For progression, add survival.

One assembled set rather than seventeen separate shopping lists, built to a single rule: reach crit immunity from gear and enchants alone, so no gem is spent on Defense. That leaves all twelve sockets free for pure Stamina, which is the highest-value gem a tank can slot. The set below lands on 497 Defense before a single gem, then spends every socket on health. Any threat the gear gives up is bought back by not paying for Defense twice.

Every gem is Stamina, so a regem can never make you crittable.

What the set costs in points. Only three slots are bought rather than looted, and all three are Arena Points:

  • Vengeful Gladiator's Plate Helm, 1,875
  • Vengeful Gladiator's Plate Chestpiece, 1,875
  • Vengeful Gladiator's Plate Legguards, 1,875

Total: 5,625 Arena Points and 0 Honor Points. Nothing else in the set touches a PvP vendor, so there is no honor grind attached to this build at all. None of the three is rating-gated either, only the shoulders at 2000 rating and the season weapons are, so this is a time cost rather than a skill wall: play your ten games a week and the points arrive whether you win or lose. Buy in the order chest, legs, helm, since you sit above the Defense cap while gearing and the chest is the piece carrying three sockets.

If you are late to the season, wait it out. Once the next season opens, this gear loses its rating requirement and is heavily discounted, so the same three pieces cost a fraction of the above. The raid-drop version below costs zero points if you would rather not touch arena at all.

Why the Defense comes from gear. The three arena pieces carry no Defense at all, so five raid slots pay for all of them: the bracers, boots, hands, second trinket and shield hold 150 of the 264 rating between them. That is the trade this build makes, and it is why no gem has to help. Because gear covers the cap on its own, all twelve sockets go to Stamina. Gemming Defense instead would mean eleven of those twelve gems stop being Stamina, which is a large amount of health traded away to free three gear slots. Pay for Defense once, in gear, and the sockets stay free.

Raid-drop version: no arena needed

If you do not play arena, the same set works from raid drops alone. Wear Faceplate of the Impenetrable, Glory of the Defender and Praetorian's Legguards in the three arena slots. They bring 65 Defense Rating of their own, so you climb to 502 Defense and can put the bracers and boots back to the threat versions.

  • What you gain: a lot of Stamina, 12 gem slots instead of 11, and no arena grind. Praetorian's Legguards alone adds three sockets the arena legs do not have.
  • What you pay: 129 Strength, 121 crit and all 106 Resilience Rating. This version is the survival build, not the threat build.
  • The catch: Faceplate of the Impenetrable drops from Illidan Stormrage, so the head slot finishes last. Until it drops, wear Onslaught Battle-Helm and cover the missing Defense with one Thick Lionseye.

Hit and expertise. Hit reaches 151 rating from items alone, past the 142 (9%) single-weapon cap with 9 to spare, so no gem or enchant is spent on it and a gear swap will not drop you under. Expertise is the one thing this set gives up. It is 62 rating, which is 15 expertise, and Defiance 3/3 adds 6, so the set sits at 21 and is 5 short of the 26 point dodge cap. An Orc on The Brutalizer gets the missing 5 from the axe racial and lands exactly on 26. Everyone else tanks with a few more dodged Shield Slams, which is the price of not needing Belt of One-Hundred Deaths to make the set work.

Block value is a threat stat here, do not read it as survival. Shield Slam adds your shield block value to its damage, which is why Gauntlets of Enforcement holds its slot over every damage glove in the phase, and why dropping Bulwark of Azzinoth costs a real 14 points of Shield Slam damage rather than nothing.

  • Sockets, 1 meta and 11 coloured (6 yellow, 5 red): Powerful Earthstorm Diamond in the meta and 11x Solid Empyrean Sapphire in every coloured socket, or Solid Star of Elune if epic gems are too expensive. Ignore the socket bonuses, none of them beat a full Stamina gem. The meta only asks for 3 blue gems, which you have twelve of.
  • Do not use Relentless Earthstorm Diamond here. It needs 2 yellow and 2 red gems active and you have none, so it simply will not turn on. It belongs to the threat version above.
  • Your Defense dial: you sit 17 Defense Rating above the minimum on Aldor, 22 on Scryers. That margin lives in gear and enchants, so no regem can take it away. The one thing that can is losing the bracer or shoulder enchant.

Gems

Gemming as Prot Warrior is straightforward: stack pure Stamina. Stamina gems carry 50% more stat budget than other primary stats, and Stamina is further boosted 5% by Vitality. Ignore most socket bonuses unless they give +3 Stamina or more. Sourced from the Wowhead Protection Warrior Tank Gems & Enchants Guide.

Budget gem: If your server's economy makes Solid Star of Elune (+12 Sta) too expensive, the rare-quality Solid Azure Moonstone (+9 Sta) is a perfectly acceptable pre-raid substitute.

Meta Gem: When to Switch

Start the expansion in Powerful Earthstorm Diamond, it's cheap, requires 3 blue gems (which you have plenty of), and the +18 Stamina is your best stat. Switch to Relentless Earthstorm Diamond later when:

  • Higher item levels and epic gems give you enough Stamina to feel safe
  • Your PvP gear and jewelry stack Crit/Agi making the +3% crit damage worthwhile
  • Hard-hitting bosses spike your rage generation, increasing Heroic Strike usage
  • You've picked up Impale for the multiplicative crit-damage scaling

Cost of Relentless: requires 2 yellow + 2 red gems active to qualify. That replaces 4 pure-stamina blues with orange/green/purple gems and is a noticeable EHP loss. Only switch when you're comfortable with your survival numbers.

Slot-Specific Notes

Head

Glyph of the Defender (Keepers of Time - Revered) is the BiS tank head enchant. Skip Glyph of Ferocity, that's a Fury enchant.

Shoulder

Aldor vs Scryers should NOT be decided on shoulder enchant alone, the difference is 0.02% avoidance. Pick whichever faction matches your other gear/utility plans. Buy from Inscriber Saalyn (Aldor) or Inscriber Veredis (Scryers) in Shattrath.

Weapon (Phase Outlook)

Mongoose dominates Phase 1. When Enchant Weapon - Executioner unlocks (Phase 3), consider running Executioner on a threat-focused mainhand and Mongoose on a survival/swap weapon.

Flasks, elixirs, food, potions, scrolls, and engineering gadgets for Protection Warrior tanking. Sourced from the Wowhead Protection Warrior Tank Buffs & Consumables Guide.

TBC consumable rules: You can have one Battle Elixir + one Guardian Elixir at the same time, OR a single Flask (which counts as both). Flasks persist through death. Scrolls do NOT count as elixirs and stack freely unless the stat overlaps with another buff (e.g. Scroll of Stamina won't stack with Prayer of Fortitude).

Defensive Debuffs (Mitigation)

These reduce damage you take. Make sure your raid covers them.

  • Thunder Clap - 10% attack speed slow (you apply)
  • Demoralizing Shout - -300 Attack Power on enemies
  • Insect Swarm (Druid) - -2% hit chance on target
  • Shadow Embrace (Warlock) - -5% physical damage dealt
  • Scorpid Sting (Hunter) - -5% melee/ranged hit

Offensive Debuffs (Threat)

These boost the damage you (and the raid) deal to your target.

  • Sunder Armor or Improved Expose Armor - -2600 armor (5 stacks)
  • Faerie Fire + Improved Faerie Fire - -610 armor + 3% more melee hit chance
  • Curse of Recklessness - -800 armor
  • Improved Seal of the Crusader - +3% crit taken
  • Mangle (Feral) - +30% bleed damage taken
  • Blood Frenzy (Arms Warrior) - +4% physical damage taken
  • Improved Hunter's Mark - +110 AP on attackers
  • Expose Weakness (Hunter) - scales AP off Hunter's Agility
  • Gift of Arthas - +8 physical damage taken (from the elixir proc)
